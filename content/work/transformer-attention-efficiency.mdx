## Abstract

Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across various natural language processing tasks. However, the quadratic computational complexity of self-attention mechanisms poses significant challenges for processing long sequences. In this work, we propose Efficient Sparse Attention (ESA), a novel attention pattern that leverages structured sparsity to reduce complexity while preserving the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies.

## Key Contributions

- **Structured Sparsity Pattern**: Introduced a learnable sparse attention mask that adapts to the input sequence structure
- **Theoretical Guarantees**: Proved that ESA maintains approximation bounds within 2% of full attention
- **Empirical Validation**: Achieved state-of-the-art results on long-document understanding benchmarks
- **Practical Efficiency**: Demonstrated 3.5x speedup on sequences with 16K+ tokens

## Results

Our experiments on SCROLLS, LongBench, and custom long-context datasets show that ESA achieves competitive performance with significantly reduced computational overhead. The model successfully processes documents up to 128K tokens on consumer-grade GPUs.
