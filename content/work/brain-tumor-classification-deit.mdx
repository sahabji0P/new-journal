## Abstract

Brain Tumors are among the most complicated and serious neurological disorders, with over 120 subtypes classified by WHO. Traditional manual examination via medical imaging proves time-consuming and error-prone, prompting the development of automated diagnostic approaches using Vision Transformers.

This research addresses brain tumor classification through transformer-based deep learning, employing the DeiT (Data-efficient Image Transformers) architecture for accurate and efficient diagnosis.

## Methodology

The study employed the **deit-small-patch16-224** variant pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned for tumor classification. The dataset comprised:

- **34,272** augmented images derived from **7,022** original MRI scans
- **Four classification categories**: glioma, meningioma, pituitary tumor, and no tumor
- **Five-fold cross-validation** for robust evaluation

## Key Results

The model achieved exceptional performance metrics:

| Metric | Score |
|--------|-------|
| Average Accuracy | 99.93% |
| Best Fold Accuracy | 99.96% (Fold 4) |
| Precision | 0.99925 |
| Recall | 0.99925 |
| F1-Score | 0.9995 |

The consistent results across folds demonstrated robust generalization with minimal overfitting.

## Future Directions

- **Multimodal Integration**: Combining MRI data with genomic and clinical records
- **Synthetic Data Generation**: GAN-based augmentation for rare tumor subtypes
- **Explainable AI**: Implementation using SHAP, LIME, and attention heatmaps
